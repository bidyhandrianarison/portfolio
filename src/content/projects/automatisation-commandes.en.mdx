## Context

A distribution company managed a planning cycle that occupied the team for approximately two weeks each month. The process involved manual supplier data extraction, complex spreadsheet calculations, and collective validation involving five people.

## Problem

The manual cycle was lengthy, prone to calculation errors, and impossible to speed up without reducing decision quality. Decision-makers waited for order suggestions while stocks fluctuated, creating risks of stockouts or overstocking.

## Approach

I built a three-stage system:

1. **Automatic data import** — Supplier data (pricing, lead times, volumes) is extracted and normalized automatically, replacing the manual extraction phase.
2. **Suggestion generation** — A model analyzes historical data, consumption trends, and logistical constraints to propose costed order suggestions.
3. **Simplified validation** — The team reviews suggestions on a unified dashboard and validates with one click, with the ability to adjust before confirmation.

## Challenges

- **Source data quality** — Supplier data arrived in heterogeneous formats (CSV, PDF, emails). A cleanup pipeline was built to normalize inputs.
- **Team trust** — Moving from collective decisions to automated suggestions required a calibration phase, where suggestions were compared to manual decisions over three historical cycles.
- **Exception handling** — Certain products require specific rules (minimum order quantities, multi-year contracts). The system integrates configurable business rules.

## Result

The planning cycle dropped from two weeks to two days. Calculation errors were eliminated. The team now dedicates their time to strategic analysis rather than data extraction.
